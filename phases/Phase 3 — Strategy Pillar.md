# Codex Execution Prompt

You are acting as a senior full-stack architect, UI engineer and implementation specialist.

Your task is to build **Phase 3 — Strategy Pillar** for the Mula Group ecosystem repository.

## Project context
Mula Group is a modular business ecosystem platform with:
- one main portal: `mulagroup.eu`
- six pillar sites:
  - strategy
  - digital
  - commerce
  - industry
  - projects
  - lifestyle

The Strategy pillar is the first and most important pillar to implement because it acts as:
- the architecture layer,
- the diagnostic layer,
- the entry point for complex multi-layered business problems,
- the bridge between broad client needs and the right ecosystem path.

This site is not a generic consulting page.
It must feel like a **premium strategic business platform**.

# PHASE 3 — STRATEGY PILLAR

## Main goal
Build the Strategy pillar homepage / core pillar page using the shared foundation and the Strategy blueprint.

The page must:
- clearly position the Strategy pillar,
- explain what type of problems it solves,
- show how Mula Strategy works,
- present services and formats,
- guide users toward the right next step,
- establish Mula Group as a serious strategic partner rather than a generic consulting firm.

The result should feel:
- premium
- calm
- intelligent
- structured
- high-trust
- practical, not abstract

---

## Scope of work
Build the main Strategy pillar page with the following sections:

### 1. Header / navigation
Create a pillar-specific navigation aligned with the shared ecosystem style.

Suggested navigation:
- Overview
- Services
- Process
- Who it’s for
- Contact

Include one primary CTA such as:
- `Book a strategy conversation`

The navigation must feel connected to the Mula Group ecosystem while still being pillar-specific.

---

### 2. Hero section
Build a Strategy hero section that communicates:
- strategic clarity,
- business architecture,
- multidimensional problem solving,
- structured direction for growth and transformation.

Include:
- label / eyebrow
- headline
- subheadline
- 1–2 CTAs
- a visual treatment aligned with the premium corporate-tech system

The hero should feel strategic, not flashy.

---

### 3. Trust / intro section
Build a section that explains:
- why this is more than generic consulting,
- why Mula Strategy focuses on structure and execution logic,
- why complex business and project situations need architectural thinking.

This section should reduce ambiguity and increase trust.

---

### 4. Services section
Build a clear, premium services section for Strategy.

It should include the core service areas from the Strategy blueprint, such as:
- Growth Strategy
- Business Architecture
- Venture Design
- Funding & Grants Pathways
- Strategic Project Planning
- Multi-domain Integration

Each service block should:
- be concise,
- be clear,
- feel business-relevant,
- avoid overloading the page.

---

### 5. Who it’s for section
Build a section that helps users self-identify.

This section should speak to use cases like:
- growing companies
- traditional businesses in transition
- founders / entrepreneurs
- investment or concept-driven projects
- multi-layered challenges needing structure

The goal is to help the right people feel immediately recognized.

---

### 6. Process / operating model section
Build the Strategy process section.

Suggested structure:
- Understand
- Diagnose
- Design
- Connect
- Activate

This section should communicate that Mula Strategy works through a method, not random advisory.

---

### 7. Offer formats section
Build a section presenting strategic engagement formats, such as:
- Strategy Session
- Business Architecture Sprint
- Venture Blueprint
- Funding Readiness
- Strategic Ecosystem Design

This section should help users understand what “working together” can look like in practice.

---

### 8. Differentiators section
Build a section explaining why Mula Strategy is different.

This should communicate:
- cross-domain strategic thinking,
- system architecture,
- execution-aware planning,
- connection to the wider ecosystem,
- ability to move from complexity to structure.

Do not make this sound like generic consulting marketing.

---

### 9. Cross-pillar logic section
Build a section that shows how Strategy connects into the wider Mula Group ecosystem.

This section should explain that once the right structure is defined, the project may continue into:
- Digital
- Commerce
- Projects
- Industry
- Lifestyle

The visual and content treatment should make the ecosystem feel coherent, not scattered.

---

### 10. FAQ section
Build an FAQ section using the Strategy blueprint logic.

It should handle common questions such as:
- what types of clients fit,
- whether this is only for large companies,
- whether this helps with grants / funding logic,
- whether implementation can follow after strategy.

---

### 11. Final CTA / contact section
Build a strong but clear final CTA section that invites:
- booking a strategy conversation
- sending project context
- starting with strategic clarity

Keep the experience premium and low-friction.

A full CRM workflow is not required yet.
A clean placeholder or basic form shell is sufficient if backend is not part of this phase.

---

### 12. Footer
Use the shared ecosystem footer logic, aligned with the Strategy pillar context.

---

## UX rules
The page must help the user quickly understand:
1. what the Strategy pillar is,
2. when it is the right entry point,
3. what kinds of problems it helps structure,
4. how working together happens,
5. what the next step should be.

The page should feel more like:
- a strategic clarity platform
than:
- a list of consulting services.

---

## Content implementation rules
Use the content and blueprint documents as the main source for:
- headlines
- service naming
- CTA language
- tone of voice
- section logic

Do not invent random messaging that drifts away from the Strategy blueprint.

---

## Design direction
The Strategy site must feel:
- premium
- strategic
- calm
- structured
- intelligent
- business-first
- corporate-tech

Avoid:
- agency clichés
- startup hype
- shallow inspirational tone
- overdesigned motion
- “innovation buzzword” overload

This is a serious high-trust pillar.
